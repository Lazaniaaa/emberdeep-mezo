# Emberdeep economy report

Generated by `npm run economy` from the same code the game runs. Every number is a simulation with bots, not a measurement of players.
The bots see only what the lantern lights ("fog" bots) and rarely die, so treat the returns as an upper bound for real players.
Money is simulated USDC. The game counts it in cents; this report shows dollars.
Descent cost = one entry key ($0.50) + lantern flasks ($0.20 each). One-off purchases (Delver mints, weapons, armor, passes) are extra spend that also goes through the split; they are not in the return columns.
Every dollar spent is split 60% round pool, 7% lock pool (67% back to players in total) and 33% studio revenue. The round pool is shared by descent gold, the lock pool by passive gold, so the total paid out is fixed: nothing here can print or drain money, and a lock can never take anything from a delver who plays.
The return cap: a delver takes back at most 160% of what they put into descents in a round (keys and oil), so the best case is +60% a round. What the cap holds back stays in the pool for the next round. Cells marked † are where the cap bound.
Strength: a perk you only hold works at ×0.6. A lock starts at ×0.8, gains ×0.05 for every round closed and stops at ×1.3. A lock is mature after 4 rounds (×1.00).

The strongest build earns well above average only while few others play it. Its edge is paid by everyone else's gold, and it shrinks as it spreads (compare the rows); if everyone has it, the round pays every delver exactly 60% again.
That is why an edge is worth buying but cannot be farmed without limit: the more delvers buy it, the less each one's gold share is worth.

## E. The lock pool: what passive farming pays

Each simulated crowd week puts $14.00 (7% of $200.00 spent) in the lock pool, shared by passive gold. The demo's crowd of lockers farms 16,000 passive gold a round; that is an assumption, like the crowd's gold per cent. Passive gold = value in cents × 0.1 × strength, so the pool follows capital, not luck.
Farmed by one lock, alone in a crowd of that size:

| Lock | Round 1 | Round 5 | Round 10 | 10 rounds in total | Per round, % of value | Payback on a mint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Common Delver ($1.00) | $0.01 | $0.01 | $0.01 | $0.09 | 0.9% | 113 rounds |
| Epic Delver ($6.00) | $0.04 | $0.05 | $0.07 | $0.54 | 0.9% | 113 rounds |
| Legendary Delver ($15.00) | $0.10 | $0.13 | $0.16 | $1.33 | 0.9% | 113 rounds |
| Stake ($50.00) | $0.34 | $0.42 | $0.53 | $4.34 | 0.9% | n/a |

Payback compares the yield to what minting the Delver cost (33% of which went to the studio). A stake is never spent, so its yield is on top of getting it back. What a lock farms is held until it is mature (4 rounds).

The pool is fixed, so more lockers means a smaller cut for each. Yield falls as the crowd of lockers grows; it cannot inflate.

### Why hopping in and out does not pay

A Legendary Delver farms about $0.10 in its first round. Breaking it before maturity forfeits half of that and costs a fee of $1.50 (10% of $15.00), roughly 14 times a round's yield. So a lock only makes sense if it is kept.


## F. Expeditions

A Delver can be sent away for $1.00 plus an optional pack (preview: 5 seconds; a live version would take hours to a day). The price is split 55% round pool and 45% studio revenue. It comes back with a haul or with nothing.
A haul is a multiplier between ×0.6 and ×15 of 1 key, 1 ticket and 120 gold; its rarity is drawn from Common 84%, Uncommon 12%, Rare 3%, Epic 0.7%, Legendary 0.1%. The average successful haul is ×1.16.
Worth: a key $0.50, a ticket $0.20 (an assumption: what a ticket is worth depends on the whole week's draw), a gold $0.00 (what one gold is worth in the demo's round pool before the return cap). Hauls are never paid in money.

| Pack | Total cost | Studio | Round pool | Returns with a haul | Average haul | Average worth | Worth ÷ cost | ×2 or more | ×5 or more | ×10 or more |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No pack | $1.00 | $0.45 | $0.55 | 45% | 0.52 keys, 0.52 tickets, 63 gold | $0.40 | 40% | 3.9% | 0.36% | 0.05% |
| Scout Pack | $1.30 | $0.59 | $0.72 | 58% | 0.67 keys, 0.67 tickets, 81 gold | $0.52 | 40% | 5.0% | 0.46% | 0.06% |
| Ranger Pack | $1.60 | $0.72 | $0.88 | 70% | 0.81 keys, 0.81 tickets, 98 gold | $0.63 | 39% | 6.0% | 0.56% | 0.07% |
| Vanguard Pack | $1.90 | $0.86 | $1.05 | 82% | 0.95 keys, 0.95 tickets, 114 gold | $0.74 | 39% | 7.1% | 0.66% | 0.08% |

Even with the best pack an average trip is worth less than it cost, so an expedition is a sink with a lottery ticket attached, not a faucet. Packs are priced so that the average worth stays at about half the cost at every tier: a pack buys a steadier trip (a higher chance of coming back with a haul), not a better deal.
The keys and tickets are game items, not money, but a key is worth what a player would otherwise pay to descend ($0.50), so the table counts them. Gold joins the round like gold from a descent and is subject to the return cap.
